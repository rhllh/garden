i decided that i wanted to have sunday to myself. i was already feeling socially exhausted but a day trip invitation to a forest and water sports - which would've been so fun, too! - felt like the perfect opportunity to just step back completely and have a more chill day.

jiyucho was the first stop of my long solo trek across tokyo. i created the itinerary on a whim the night before; places to scout for a group project, potential shopping locations scattered in between. i only had one restaurant saved and that turned out to be a mistake. let's always prioritise nourishing our bodies whenever during our travels!

jiyucho (自由丁) is located in kuramae, near asakusa and is a warmly lit space for you to be with yourself. its name comes from 'Jiyu-cho/自由帳', which means 'free notebook', the kind that you would use to express any and all of your unfiltered thoughts. it was opened in 2019 by poet and artist shohei koyama (小山将平). <span class="inline-side-note">after a couple of hurdles<span class="inline-side-note__text"><strong>interview</strong><br /><em>accepting 'i don't know' and living 'as myself' without straining. | jiyucho owner shohei koyama</em> by robert shimokitazawa [(link)](https://note.com/robertsmkt/n/n856d62e37061?hl=en)</span></span> to starting a professional career, he found himself one day holed up in a hotel room reading through his past poems and essays. they contained worries and concerns he had at that point of time, and he felt encouraged realising that a year from now, this moment would just be a lesson to his future self as well. and so, this physical store was born, from the resolve to bring that same experience to others.

![View of the street from inside Jiyucho](/garden/photos/tokyo-2026/jiyucho/jiyucho-2.JPG)

although this visit was haphazardly planned, i was the first walk-in customer on that sunday morning, and was lucky enough to be able to do the 'tomoboshi letter' <span class="inline-side-note">activity<span class="inline-side-note__text"><strong>how to spend time</strong><br />other activities: essay + drink, coworking + drink, browse for a fee [(link)](https://jiyucho.tokyo/en/pages/jiyucho-tokyo)</span></span>. this was a letter set, which includes a 'scene' of your choice, a drink, a card with reflection prompts, a postcard for yourself and a postcard for a loved one. 

it begins with setting the 'scene': a specific postcard design on one side and a short essay written by shohei koyama on the other. you naturally begin to reflect on the words as you read them, and on how you're feeling in that moment. the prompts card was a great warming-up activity as well to get the writing juices flowing.

![The TOMOBOSHI LETTER set](/garden/photos/tokyo-2026/jiyucho/jiyucho-3.JPG)

you then move on to the blank postcard, to start writing to your future self. for me, i was reflecting on my first week in tokyo; i was inspired and overwhelmed by all the creativity and had a lot of uncertainty hanging over my head. i asked my future self whether all of my worries would be resolved in one way or another by the time she reads the letter. i didn't think i would have so much to say, but it ended up becoming a journal entry of sorts, which will remain in tokyo for a little while after i leave.

you can take your time writing it, and you're free to use different pens, washi tape and stamps available by the reception area. flip through the books around the shop, or admire the wall of postcards and scrap notes left behind by previous customers. if the words escape you, add a drawing or some colours instead. there's no pressure to complete anything; it's just you for a moment in time. at the end, i wrote my address and picked 4 pellets to create a wax seal for the envelope, and that's it.

![4 wax pellets melting](/garden/photos/tokyo-2026/jiyucho/jiyucho-4.JPG)

a year ago i definitely could not have imagined myself in tokyo, doing a writing course and feeling inspired enough to send my future self a letter. so i'm really looking forward to seeing where i'll be in a year's time, and what i'll be thinking about when i read it.
